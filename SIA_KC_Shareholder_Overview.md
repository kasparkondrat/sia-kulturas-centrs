## 1. Company Structure & Role

SIA KC is a commercial entity that holds the primary lease for Skolas iela 15 from FL. Under the current operating model (Scenario 2), KC manages only the 1st floor and basement (246.68 m² of 746.46 m² total building area, ~33%).

The remaining 2nd and 3rd floors (499.78 m², ~67%) are operated by Biedrība "Kaņepes Laikmetīgās Kultūras Centrs" (KLKC), a separate non-profit entity with its own lease with FL. KLKC runs cultural programming, corporate event rentals, and coworking — and is the recipient of the ERAF project (Nr. 4.3.2.0/1/25/A/044, €848k EU funding).

KC's role under Scenario 2 is narrower than historical: it acts as the commercial landlord subletting 1F to the bar/restaurant tenant, holds building-level utility contracts, and carries all legacy loan obligations.

### Why Two Entities?

The split exists because KLKC (as a non-profit) can access EU structural fund grants (ERAF) and other public programme funding that a commercial SIA cannot. KC retains the commercial sublease income and the relationship with tenants. The split also ring-fences loan risk: KLKC has zero loan obligations.

## 2. Historical Context (2023–2025)

### 2023 — Sarūsējis Sublease Signed

On 28 June 2023, KC signed a 100-month sublease with SIA "Sarūsējis" (Reg. Nr. 40203413041, board member Nauris Krēsliņš) for the 1st floor:

- Area: 201.78 m² indoor + 37.74 m² basement + 282.85 m² land (terrace)
- Fixed rent: €4,200/month
- Variable rent: 4.2% of monthly turnover exceeding €45,000
- Term: 100 months from signing (~June 2023 to October 2031)

This sublease is KC's sole significant revenue source. Variable rent at base level (€3,790/month) assumes ~€90,000 monthly turnover for Sarūsējis, with 2% annual growth modelled from 2028.

### 2024 — Transition Year

2024 was a transition year. KLKC reported a loss of -€3,105 with cash reserves of only €5,085 at year-end. The 1st floor was under renovation for Sarūsējis buildout. KC's own financials for this period are not separately available in the current document set, but the building was operating at reduced capacity.

Key events:
- 1F renovation underway for Sarūsējis fit-out
- 2F and 3F not yet operational (pre-ERAF)
- Building costs were borne primarily by KC under the old structure

### 2025 — Recovery and ERAF Approval

2025 marked the start of financial recovery:
- Sarūsějis became fully operational, generating stable revenue
- KLKC's earned revenue from events reached ~€170,000 (tickets + commissions)
- Total KLKC income (all sources): ~€200,000
- KLKC expenses: ~€200,000 (breakeven)
- ERAF project approved (Lēmums 02.03.2026, Nr. 39-2-10/1563) with conditions
- All CFLA conditions fulfilled by April 2026 deadline

At current 60-month terms:
- **Annual loan service: ~€86,400** (principal + interest)
- **Annual EBITDA: ~€59,000–65,000**
- **DSCR: 0.69x** — KC cannot cover its debt service from operating cash flow

The off-books loan (€66,911) has no interest and no amortization schedule. It sits as a perpetual liability on the balance sheet.

### The Core Problem

At 60-month terms, KC runs a cumulative cash deficit of **-€137,764** through 2031 (when both loans are repaid). KC only turns cumulative-positive in **2034** (year 9 of the model). By model end (April 2041), cumulative cash is +€500,694 — but the first 8 years require external financing or restructuring to survive.

This is not viable without renegotiation.

## 8. Loan-to-Equity Conversion Options

An alternative to renegotiating terms is converting part or all of the loan principal into equity in SIA KC.

### Loan Pool Composition

| Lender | Principal | Share of pool |
|--------|-----------|--------------|
| Rododendri | €182,000 | 42.1% |
| SALMO/GV | €250,000 | 57.9% |
| **Total convertible** | **€432,000** | **100%** |

The off-books loan (€66,911) is excluded from conversion scenarios as it has different characteristics (no interest, no schedule, potentially a different category of obligation).

### Conversion Scenarios

**Full conversion (both loans → equity):**
- €432,000 debt eliminated
- Rododendri receives 42.1% equity stake
- SALMO/GV receives 57.9% equity stake
- KC becomes debt-free (except off-books €66,911)
- All EBITDA (~€59,000–65,000/year) available as distributable cash or reinvestment
- Current founder/shareholder dilution: significant

**Rododendri-only conversion (€182,000 → equity):**
- SALMO/GV keeps €250,000 as debt (renegotiated terms)
- Rododendri gets equity, aligns incentives as co-owner
- Remaining debt service: ~€2,604/month at 96mo or ~€2,083/month at 120mo
- DSCR improves substantially (~1.8x at 96mo, ~2.4x at 120mo)

**SALMO/GV-only conversion (€250,000 → equity):**
- Rododendri keeps €182,000 as debt (renegotiated terms)
- SALMO/GV gets equity, reducing debt burden significantly
- Remaining debt service: ~€1,896/month at 96mo or ~€1,517/month at 120mo

### Valuation Context

Equity conversion requires agreement on company valuation. Rough valuation approaches:

| Method | Multiple | Enterprise Value | Equity Value (EV − debt) |
|--------|----------|-----------------|-------------------------|
| 3x EBITDA | 3x | €189,000 | -€310,000 |
| 4x EBITDA | 4x | €252,000 | -€247,000 |
| 5x EBITDA | 5x | €315,000 | -€184,000 |
| 6x EBITDA | 6x | €378,000 | -€121,000 |

**At any standard EBITDA multiple (3–6x), KC's equity value is negative.** The total debt (€499k) exceeds any reasonable enterprise value. This means:

1. Converting debt to equity at face value would give lenders majority or total ownership
2. Any equity conversion should be negotiated at a discount to principal, reflecting the lenders' actual recovery prospects
3. The lenders' alternative (enforcing the loan) has poor recovery prospects given KC's negative equity position — this is leverage for negotiation

### What Lenders Should Consider

At current 60-month terms, lenders face a choice:
- **Enforce terms:** KC cannot pay. Default risk is near-certain by year 2–3.
- **Extend terms:** Lenders get repaid in full + interest, but over 8–10 years. Total recovery: €533,000–566,000 (principal + interest).
- **Convert to equity:** Lenders become co-owners of a company with ~€59,000–65,000 annual EBITDA. At a 10% yield target, their €432,000 investment returns ~14% annually from operating cash flow. But realization (exit) is uncertain — there is no liquid market for KC shares.

The strongest argument for lenders to extend terms (rather than convert) is that they receive guaranteed interest income and priority over equity holders. Conversion only makes sense if they believe KC's EBITDA will grow substantially or if there is a credible exit path.

## 10. Key Risks & Assumptions

### Critical Risks

1. **Single-tenant dependency.** 100% of revenue comes from Sarūsějis. No diversification possible within 1F.

2. **Sublease term.** The 100-month sublease expires approximately October 2031. If Sarūsějis does not renew (or renegotiates at lower rates), all projections beyond 2031 are void. The model assumes continuation at current terms indefinitely — this is optimistic.

3. **Negative equity.** KC's total debt (~€499k) exceeds any reasonable enterprise value. The company is technically insolvent on a balance sheet basis even while generating positive EBITDA.

4. **No capital reserves.** At 60-month terms, KC accumulates no cash reserves during the loan repayment period. Any unexpected expense (major repair, legal dispute, tenant default) has no buffer.

5. **FL lease dependency.** KC's entire business depends on the lease with FL. Lease terms, rent changes, or building disposition are outside KC's control.

### Model Assumptions

- No rent escalation to FL (both KC and KLKC rents modelled flat)
- No inflation on operating costs (utilities, maintenance, admin are flat)
- Variable rent grows 2% annually from 2028
- Loan interest: 4% annual, compounded monthly
- No CAPEX modelled (facade works line is zero)
- Off-books loan: no repayment, no interest, perpetual
- Communal utility costs are flat (no seasonal variation modelled)

### Data Gaps

This analysis is limited by available documentation. The following would strengthen the shareholder assessment:

- **KC audited financial statements** (2022–2025) — actual P&L, balance sheets, cash flow statements
- **Historical Sarūsějis turnover data** — to validate the variable rent assumption (€3,790/month base implies ~€90k/month turnover)
- **FL lease terms document** — to confirm rent stability and renewal conditions
- **KC share register** — current ownership structure and any existing shareholder agreements
- **Off-books loan terms** — counterparty, conditions, any conversion triggers
- **Actual arrears balance** — to determine when the €147.53/month obligation ends

## 11. Strategic Considerations

### For Current Shareholders

KC's value proposition is straightforward: it holds a profitable sublease and the primary lease on a well-located Rīga property. The business generates ~€60,000–65,000 annual EBITDA. The problem is the debt overhang — nearly €500,000 against a company that, on any reasonable valuation basis, is worth less than that.

**Recommended priorities:**

1. **Renegotiate loan terms immediately.** 60-month terms are not viable. Minimum: 96 months (DSCR 1.1x). Preferred: 120 months (DSCR 1.37x). The additional interest cost (€40k–73k over the model period) is the price of survival.

2. **Consider partial conversion selectively.** If a lender is willing to convert debt to equity, this should be done at a discount to principal reflecting KC's negative equity position. A lender converting €250,000 of debt at face value is overpaying — they should be getting at most 50–60 cents on the euro in equity terms.

3. **Address the sublease renewal.** The Sarūsějis sublease expires in ~2031. Well before that, KC should either negotiate extension terms or develop a contingency plan. Losing this tenant with loans still outstanding would be catastrophic.

4. **Clarify the off-books loan.** €66,911 sitting as a perpetual zero-interest liability creates uncertainty. This should either be formally written off, converted to equity at agreed terms, or given a repayment schedule.

### For Potential Lender-Shareholders (Rododendri, SALMO/GV)

Converting debt to equity means giving up guaranteed interest income and creditor priority in exchange for an ownership stake in a small, single-tenant property management company. The case for conversion rests on:

- Belief that KC's EBITDA will grow beyond current projections
- Desire to influence company strategy (as owner vs. creditor)
- Recognition that enforcing the current loan terms would likely trigger default, producing worse recovery than conversion at a discount
- Long-term alignment with the Skolas 15 cultural project ecosystem

The case against: creditor position is legally stronger, interest income is guaranteed if terms are extended, and there is no clear exit path for equity in a private Latvian SIA.

## Appendix: Relationship Between KC and KLKC

For shareholders evaluating KC, it is important to understand how the two entities interact:

| Flow | Direction | Amount | Nature |
|------|-----------|--------|--------|
| Rent to FL | KC → FL | €863/mo | KC's lease obligation |
| Rent to FL | KLKC → FL | €1,869/mo | KLKC's separate lease |
| Utility recharge | KLKC → KC | €940/mo | KLKC's 67% share of communals |
| Operating costs | Each entity pays own | varies | 33/67 split by area |
| Loan service | KC only | €7,200/mo (60mo) | KLKC has zero loans |
| Sarūsějis contribution | Sarūsějis → KLKC | €2,500/mo | Separate from KC sublease |

KC and KLKC are financially independent. KLKC's ERAF project, grants, and cultural revenue do not flow to KC. KC's debt does not burden KLKC. The only financial link is the utility recharge (€940/month from KLKC to KC).

This independence is by design: it protects KLKC's grant eligibility and ring-fences KC's commercial risk.
